# Artifact Domain Semantics v0.1 — proposed

**Status: proposed for owner review, not implemented or qualified.** This is a design and synthetic case set for [Issue #5](https://github.com/kmosoti/CEGAR/issues/5), not an API, collector, compiler, execution engine, proof of model fidelity, or change to Issue #2. The existing finite checker checks a supplied graph only; neither its result nor anything here grants execution authority. The proposal follows [the formal model](FORMAL_MODEL.md), [contracts](CONTRACTS.md), and [security boundary](SECURITY.md); the choices and alternatives are recorded in [ADR 0007](../knowledge/decisions/0007-artifact-domain-semantics.md).

## Purpose and explicit scope

Check the safety properties **every modeled ready stage/test/publish attempt satisfies its required preconditions**, and **every modeled publish attempt is for a staged immutable artifact, covered by a passing applicable registered test and authorized by the bound policy**. Record the exact checked rule set. A successful verification does not imply a publish occurs or that the requested task succeeds. A failure that stops the plan can satisfy safety vacuously with respect to publication, but only when initial facts are consistent and all permitted paths and failures in the stated model are represented through their first violation or nonviolating termination. Report termination/failure coverage and do not call that goal completion.

A candidate analysis may simulate future stage and test outcomes. Those are **hypotheses about modeled operations**, not observations that a test actually passed. A successful modeled test can make a *modeled* attestation available to a later modeled publish, only on its pass branch and under the bound tool/collector assumptions. This proves a conditional sequence property, not the actual existence or authenticity of a future attestation. Initial external facts, including any already existing attestation, require validated observations; absent required initial evidence is not filled in by a hypothetical test or a model prediction. A future runtime must independently acquire and validate actual evidence.

## Proposed typed vocabulary

These are conceptual Rust-like types, not implemented interfaces or JSON contracts. Each `Ref<K>` is a typed, namespace-separated immutable identity, with versioned canonical encoding, algorithm identifier and content digest, resolved and checked at the trusted boundary. Equality is typed equality of algorithm and digest over canonical bytes, not a path, tag, name or unqualified 64-character string. Hash algorithm, canonical encoding, domain separation, byte-level verification and collision-resistance assumptions require owner selection; reject unsupported algorithms and ambiguous encodings, never silently coerce. Keys, time, issuer and storage identity do not become trustworthy merely because they are hashed.

- `ArtifactId { content: Digest, format: FormatId }` identifies exact bytes and format; `Base`, `Patch`, `Candidate` are typed roles, not interchangeable even when byte digests coincide. A declared candidate identity is a plan claim, not proof that a real transformation produces it. A producer output slot binds, on each modeled success branch, to that branch's actual modeled `ArtifactId`; all downstream references resolve through that binding. Validation of actual bytes and reproducible transformation is outside this design.
- `PlanId`, `NodeId`, `OperationRef { id, version, contract_digest }`, `SuiteRef { id, version, definition_digest }`, `DestinationId`, and `PrincipalId` are distinct typed values. Principal is the authenticated, scoped identity, not a plan string. A node has unique ID, operation reference, typed inputs/output slots, and dependency IDs; references resolve exactly once to snapshot entries or unique producer outputs. No arbitrary shell, path or tool name has operation authority.
- `PolicyRef`, `SnapshotRef`, `ToolRegistryRef` (including each operation contract), `SuiteRegistryRef`, `CompilerRef` and `VerifierRef` bind immutable content/implementation versions. Policy is operator-controlled and selects authorized principal, operations, suite(s), destinations, trusted issuers, freshness/revocation and applicable constraints. A version mismatch or unresolved version does not inherit authority from an older one.
- `Claim<T> { value, source_ref }` is an untrusted assertion; `Prediction<T> { value, model/runtime/template refs, optional confidence, source_ref }` is advisory. Neither is evidence of truth, and confidence never creates permission. `Observation<T>` is an internal capability/validated value created **only** by a future authenticated, authorized collector or route, not by deserializing any `kind`, signature-looking field, or flag from a plan. Verification of an externally supplied signed envelope, if eventually supported, requires an independently reviewed trust root and collector-side authentication/validation; this document defines no protocol or implementation.
- An observation's provenance includes exact source and immutable revision, collector identity and version, issuer/principal and authorization scope, integrity/authentication validation, observation and validity times, snapshot binding and evidence content identity. A `TestAttestation` additionally binds exact subject `ArtifactId`, registered `SuiteRef`, outcome (`pass`, `fail`, `error`), test-run identity, responsible runner and issuer, evidence-owning principal, tenant, intended audience/scope, time/validity, and applicable policy/tool/snapshot context. The evidence-owning principal, runner, issuer and authenticated verification/publish principal are separate roles and fields, not necessarily different identities; their values may coincide. Equality of the evidence-owning and verification/publish principals or an explicit policy-permitted sharing relation must be established. Its own digest identifies the record, not the tested artifact. A pass applies only if all required context, trust, suite, status, freshness and policy checks succeed. Identical subject digest alone is insufficient.
- `VerificationContext` pins plan, principal, policy, snapshot, registries/contracts and relevant suite(s), compiler and verifier identities, trusted validation time, canonicalization, property version, schedule/failure model, horizon and budgets. A trust decision on source/key scope, revocation, observation consistency and expiry is part of input validation. The pure compiler receives validated immutable values and explicit time, not ambient I/O or a clock; how the collector authenticates and pins these is unresolved future work.

Freshness is policy-defined: compare the supplied authenticated evaluation time against bounded validity intervals, snapshot and policy versions, trust/revocation data and operation-specific rules. The initial context must remain coherent for the modeled horizon under **explicit** stability assumptions; time progression, revocation or policy changes are not silently treated as impossible. If they may occur and are not modeled, unqualified scope is inconclusive. Expired or superseded observations/policy/snapshot are stale; mutually incompatible authoritative records for the same scoped fact are contradictory, not merged by newest-wins unless a reviewed policy explicitly defines it. Unknown currentness or authenticity is not known true. None of these checks is implemented here.

## Plan and operation semantics

Validation rejects malformed identities, duplicate IDs/producers, bad types/arity, unresolved or cyclic dependencies, unsupported operations/versions, unregistered suites, and unsupported resource constructors. References to produced values must have a dependency path from their unique producer; this does not itself guarantee that the output exists after failure. A recognized but unauthorized attempt is not removed as an invalid node solely to make the remaining graph safe: either reject the *whole* candidate with a diagnostic before compilation, or, as proposed here for known policy/precondition violations, preserve its reachable attempt as a bad state. Never remove an invalid action and verify the remainder as if it were the submitted plan.

For v0.1 the proposed schedule consists of a finite DAG, at most one attempt per node, and an explicit plan-level failure disposition. Each action below is atomic at the model boundary. A node becomes ready once all dependencies have **succeeded**; independent ready nodes may be chosen in any order. A dependency failure causes the dependent node to be `blocked`, not silently successful. `abort_all` additionally stops all not-yet-started actions after any failure; `continue_independent` permits unrelated ready actions to continue. After each action, deterministically propagate `blocked` from every failed or blocked dependency to pending dependents to a fixed point, and for `abort_all` mark all remaining pending nodes blocked. This closure is finite and order-independent on the validated finite DAG. In this v0.1 scheduler a state is terminal only when every node is succeeded, failed or blocked. A fully specified true terminal deadlock, for example when all remaining nodes are blocked and no transition is permitted, may contribute to a scoped safety result if all other paths and transitions are covered; it is not successful goal completion or evidence of liveness. After closure, an unexplained pending/no-ready stall or a permitted but unspecified transition yields `inconclusive` for the requested scope, never a silently terminal state. A request for liveness or eventual completion is also `inconclusive` unless that property and its required behavior are separately specified and checked. These semantics are part of the declared, reviewed scheduler contract, not an inferred guard that prevents an otherwise permitted publish. Missing/unsupported disposition, retries, cancellation races, in-flight actions, partial effects, or non-atomic effects require a broader model or an inconclusive result for that scope. Atomic ordering covers all serial permutations of ready actions, not arbitrary real parallel execution. An unqualified concurrency claim requires evidence that every relevant real interleaving and failure refines the model; without it report only the atomic schedule restriction or inconclusive for the requested broader scope.

A scheduled attempt is never suppressed just because its operation precondition is false. Dependencies and explicit scheduler disposition control readiness; when ready, an attempt with a known false safety precondition enters `Bad`, even if a real tool might reject it. This intentionally checks unsafe attempts as well as successful side effects. A policy may impose additional constraints; any omitted binding requirement makes compilation inconclusive, not verified.

**First-violation monitor (proposed).** `Bad` is a terminal monitor state, not an assertion that the real scheduler or operation halts. On a ready unsafe attempt, emit an explicit edge labeled with the node and attempt to `Bad`, retaining the pre-attempt state and all violated rule identifiers for deterministic replay; do not emit further edges from that monitor state or repeat the attempt. If an initial condition itself violates a modeled rule, mark its initial monitor state bad. Enumerate all permitted behavior from each non-`Bad` prefix through its first violation, and all permitted nonviolating successors; do not omit an unsafe attempt merely because the monitor stops afterward. A longer real execution with a first violation is represented by its prefix ending at that violation, not its post-violation continuation. This first-bad reduction is valid for the stated safety reachability question, not a claim of post-violation effects, complete real traces, liveness, or goal completion. Those questions require separately specified models.

| Operation | Preconditions on a ready attempt | Modeled outcomes and effects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `stage_patch(base, patch) -> candidate` | Inputs resolve to present immutable base/patch, actor and operation permitted, and contract and scope are resolved. | `success`: bind output slot to the exact identity of **each** permitted successful outcome on its respective branch, and mark that identity staged. `failure_no_effect`: no candidate, node failed; disposition applies. No claim that output bytes were independently computed. |
| `run_registered_tests(candidate, suite) -> attestation` | Candidate staged; suite is registered and this test operation and suite are permitted under policy; authorized runner/operation and correct subject. Permission to run a suite is separate from its applicability to a later publish. | `pass`: create a **hypothetical** modeled attestation with the branch's actual subject, suite, runner, issuer, validity and pinned context; do not assume it satisfies `PolicyValid` just because the runner is authorized or the outcome says pass. `fail` or `error`: no passing attestation, node failed; disposition applies. Real pass provenance must be checked later. |
| `publish(candidate, attestation, destination)` | Candidate staged; designated attestation satisfies the same scoped `PolicyValid` predicate below, whether it is an observed record or hypothetical output; authenticated principal is authorized for operation and destination; all other bound policy conditions hold. | On satisfied preconditions, `success` records a modeled publication of that exact ID; `failure_no_effect` records no publication and node failure. A false precondition gives an attempted-publish `Bad` state, not a pruned edge. No effect is a real authorization. |

**Stage output coverage.** Hard-wiring a declared candidate on success is sound for the requested scope only if the pinned contract and validated facts establish that *every* permitted successful outcome has that singleton identity. Otherwise enumerate every possible output identity and binding, and all permitted downstream successors up to first `Bad`, for each successful branch. Neither a declaration nor a digest equality establishes this guarantee. If the possible identities/bindings are unknown, unbounded, incomplete or exceed the graph limits, the requested broader result is `inconclusive` with missing facts or limit diagnostics; never silently choose the favorable identity. A separately requested conditional scenario may restrict outcomes only with a clearly bound, satisfiable assumption, identify omitted outputs, and state that coverage beyond that condition is not established. Unknown whether the restriction reflects actual behavior is not proof that it does.

**One proposed policy-validity predicate.** `PolicyValid(attestation, candidate, principal, destination, context, state)` holds only if the designated attestation has pass status; exact subject identity equals the candidate; suite ID, version and definition satisfy all bound policy requirements; the test-run, runner and collector identities/versions/scopes are authorized for that test; the issuer and its authentication/integrity chain are trusted and authorized for this record and scope; (the evidence-owning principal equals the authenticated verification/publish principal OR a specific policy rule permits that cross-principal use); AND the attestation's tenant, audience, destination and scope are compatible with the requesting principal, requested use and bound policy in **both** cases; validity intervals, policy-defined time, revocation and freshness are satisfied at the modeled attempt; and snapshot, policy and tool-contract context are compatible. Every other binding policy condition must also hold. These are proposed obligations, not a newly approved policy or trust protocol. For an initial record, these facts must derive from the validated collector boundary. For a hypothetical future pass, evaluate the **same** predicate against explicit branch values and pinned policy/context, with the required issuer, collector, provenance and temporal facts supported by validated contracts/facts or disclosed, satisfiable scope assumptions. No field gains authority merely by appearing on a modeled pass: a true hypothetical predicate is conditional on those assumptions, not a real observation or authorization.

For either kind, a known false predicate at a reachable publish yields `Bad` (or reject the whole plan under a recorded uniform rule); a known invalid test outcome may also be bad if its contract requires validity. An unknown required fact must not be promoted to true: if bounded exhaustive alternatives are supported, model each, including any false branch leading to an attempted bad publish; otherwise return `needs_evidence` for a supplyable missing fact or `inconclusive` for missing behavior/coverage. Conditional claims must disclose their restrictions and cannot stand for the unqualified requested scope.

Each outcome set is an explicit **assumption**, not a claim that real tools have only these outcomes. For example, publish failure after a partial side effect, external artifact mutation or revocation during an action cannot be represented by `failure_no_effect`; absent a reviewed faithful representation, requested coverage including it is inconclusive. Policies for stage/test denial can choose whole-plan rejection or model the unsafe attempt, but must be uniform, recorded, and cannot discard it then return positive for the original plan. A declared fail-fast controller must actually enforce the above blocking/cancellation semantics to support an eventual real-world argument.

## Deterministic compilation obligations

A future compiler is trusted safety-critical code. It must be independently reviewed and checked against an independently encoded concrete domain oracle; `Model::new` validates graph shape, not semantic completeness. Proposed algorithm, for a fixed validated context and scope:

1. Validate all identities, bindings, DAG and evidence. Establish nonempty, consistent initial states; no arbitrary assumption turns missing facts into true. A request may intentionally ask for a restricted scenario only if the restriction is explicit, satisfiable and bound into the receipt. State exactly what is excluded; an omitted possible initial state is not evidence of its impossibility.
2. Build states as canonical tuples of each node's status (`pending`, `succeeded`, `failed`, `blocked`), branch-specific output-slot bindings, available staged IDs, modeled/validated attestation records with their bindings and statuses, publications, failure disposition, and any relevant policy/freshness facts. Preserve associations: use a fixed tuple schema indexed by the sorted `NodeId` list or sorted `(NodeId, status)` pairs, and keyed output/evidence records with their producer/slot or identity. Never sort bare status values or merge `A:failed/B:pending` with `A:pending/B:failed`. Include a terminal first-violation monitor record identifying the source state, attempted node and violated rule(s). Initial state(s) encode all permitted initial alternatives; no empty initial set. Each state must preserve every fact necessary to determine readiness, outcomes and `Bad`; no merging that changes them.
3. From each non-`Bad` state enumerate every ready node and every outcome and output binding allowed by its pinned contract and disposition; add an explicit terminal `Bad` edge for every false-precondition attempt. Preserve all independent ready-node permutations up to the first violation, not just the lexicographically first schedule. Compute blocked closure after every nonviolating transition, model failures and blocked dependents explicitly, and distinguish fully specified terminal deadlocks from unexplained pending/no-ready stalls as described above. For fixed horizon `H`, augment state with step count and include every nonviolating prefix of at most `H` attempts and every first-`Bad` prefix of at most `H` attempts, including bad endpoints at `H`; states at `H` have no further edges **only for that bounded prefix claim**. An exhausted finite DAG without a horizon means full reachability of the first-violation monitor in the stated atomic model, not complete post-violation traces. If a requested full-scope analysis has reachable work beyond `H`, report inconclusive for that full scope, not unqualified safe. Zero horizon proves no future attempt and must be labeled accordingly.
4. Deterministically canonicalize all typed values using a versioned, unambiguous encoding; reject duplicate/ambiguous normalized keys. Order input identities, node IDs, operation/outcome tags, keyed records and complete state tuples by a specified total byte order of their canonical encodings (not hash-map iteration, locale or arrival order); tuple field order and node associations are fixed, never independently resorted. Enumerate to closure with sorted initial tuples, then sorted `(source tuple, node, complete outcome/bindings, target tuple)` edges; deduplicate only identical complete semantic tuples/edges. Sort the complete state tuples and assign dense IDs `0..n-1`; sort distinct full `(node ID, operation ref, outcome tag, outcome bindings)` labels and assign deterministic `u32` action IDs; sort transitions by `(source ID, action ID, target ID)`. If label mapping, ordering, or encoding cannot be represented unambiguously, reject/inconclusive rather than silently alias. Retain the inverse maps and all binding metadata for replay and audit. Actions are trace labels, never capabilities.
5. Do not silently truncate enumeration. If more than **128 states** or **512 transitions** would be required, stop and return `inconclusive` with the relevant limit and consumed counts; do not send a partial graph for a positive result. Also bound input sizes, canonicalization, exploration work, memory, witness output, and verifier refinements/work; cancellation, timeout, exhaustion or unavailable worker never yields success. Check limits while generating as well as before calling the core.
6. Pass dense initial and bad IDs and every generated `(source, action, target)` to `Model::new`. The compiler must demonstrate initial-state coverage and a simulation of every execution in the declared scope through its first violation (or its full nonviolating prefix) into the graph. From each non-`Bad` prefix, cover every permitted next transition and outcome, mapping each possible first unsafe attempt to a `Bad` endpoint. For this proposed exact monitor graph, also justify that every emitted non-`Bad` edge corresponds to a permitted modeled step, and every edge to `Bad` corresponds to the represented permitted unsafe attempt and violated rule(s). Post-`Bad` transitions are intentionally absent, not asserted impossible in the underlying domain. The core's quotient/refinement must preserve the generated graph; the domain compiler cannot assume core shape checks verify its own obligations. No refinement or repair may weaken policy.
7. On a core counterexample, replay the complete labeled witness with the core and independently against domain transitions and pinned context, decode it via retained maps, and report the violated rule and modeled assumptions. If replay or binding fails, return `inconclusive`/internal error, not a concrete violation. On core `VerifiedWithinModel`, require successful validation, complete enumeration for the requested scope and all coverage obligations; bind exact graph identity and mapping plus all context. The core itself still reports `supplied_finite_model_only`: a future wrapper may describe its independently established domain scope but must not relabel an arbitrary supplied graph as trusted.

For example, take a valid context with B already staged and a three-node chain that stages A, tests A/S1 and then attempts to publish B with the test output. Its concrete graph contains the path `initial --stage(success,A)--> staged_A --test(pass,A,S1)--> passed_A --publish(attempt,B)--> bad(subject_mismatch)`. Stage failure, test fail/error and any other allowed outcomes are additional edges, not replaced by this path. A publish of A instead takes success/no-effect-failure edges only if every other precondition holds. These names illustrate semantic tuples and labels; dense IDs come from the total ordering above, not from this diagram. Replay checks the actual transitions and context; the diagram alone is not an implementation or evidence.

A receipt must identify the exact plan and graph digest, principal and all policy/snapshot/registry/suite/tool/compiler/verifier versions, canonical encoding, property and query, first-violation monitor semantics and its safety-only limit, initial facts/provenance, modeled stage-output alternatives and bindings, supporting contract/facts and explicit conditional assumptions, hypothetical policy-validity assumptions, scheduler and failure coverage, `H` or exhausted finite model, limits/consumption, validation time/freshness assumptions, and exclusions. It must state `execution_authorized = false`. The model identity includes the ID/action mapping and complete edge/initial/bad sets; changing a bound input requires a new analysis. A positive result is only absence of `Bad` in that complete **specified** graph, conditional on compiler correctness and stated assumptions; it does not certify real-world fidelity, tool behavior or a live decision. Any future executor needs separate live authorization, integrity, freshness and race-resistant rechecks.

## Proposed result classification

The current core has only verified, concrete counterexample and inconclusive; the following is a proposed domain wrapper, not a claim about the core. Select deterministically in this order before a graph is accepted (collect bounded diagnostics within a category):

1. `invalid_input`: malformed/inconsistent structural plan, unsupported identity or operation, missing dependency, cycle, incompatible types, unregistered suite, or contradictory authenticated premises. No analysis of a repaired subset. A forged envelope explicitly presented as authentic may be rejected as invalid; untrusted text alone is merely a claim.
2. `stale_snapshot`: known expired/superseded required snapshot, policy or evidence (list exact refs). Contradictory authoritative evidence is `invalid_input`, not resolved by choosing one. If authenticity/currentness itself is unknown rather than known stale, use `needs_evidence` or `inconclusive` as below.
3. `needs_evidence`: a required initial observation, authorization, attestation, validation chain, or time binding is absent/unverifiable but can in principle be supplied. Never treat a prediction or client flag as that evidence. This is not a finding that an actual violation occurred.
4. `inconclusive`: requested semantics/coverage are unsupported or incomplete, assumptions cannot be established, graph or work limit, timeout, cancellation, failed replay, or unknown verifier answer. `unavailable` is reserved for infrastructure unavailable before/during the request, not an authorization decision. If multiple categories occur, report all safely bounded diagnostics; the above precedence does not imply later checks passed. A missing failure model must not become safe because an incomplete graph has no bad path.
5. Only after those gates: replayed reachable `Bad` gives `concrete_counterexample` **in the modeled system**; no reachable bad state with complete checking of the declared scope gives `verified_within_model`. Neither is an assertion about an actual event. Do not convert a malformed plan or a missing premise into a fabricated concrete trace. For multiple modeled violations, canonical graph/order and checker configuration fix witness selection; budgets may change a result to inconclusive.

## Synthetic cases for review

All identifiers below are synthetic. Unless stated otherwise, assume consistent fresh authenticated initial inputs and policy, registered suite `S1`, authorized principal `P1`, destination `D`, complete atomic interleavings, all listed failure branches, and enough budget. `A` and `B` are distinct exact artifact IDs. Tests and outputs here are illustrative specifications, **not executed fixtures**. The listed category is for the stated conditions, not an implemented result.

| Case | Expected proposed result and reason |
| --- | --- |
| Stage A → test A/S1 → publish A with success dependencies | `verified_within_model` for the full stated atomic safety model only if stage's singleton A is guaranteed in scope, modeled pass is policy-valid under supported assumptions, every failure blocks dependent publish, and all paths are covered; pass is hypothetical, not a real attestation. Failure branches may stop progress. |
| Stage A → test A/S1 → publish B, where B is staged, and publish is ready | `concrete_counterexample`: attempted publish subject differs from the passing attestation; exact A/B mismatch is a bad state, not a missing edge. If B is not staged, the staged precondition also fails. |
| Two unsafe ready attempts U and V can be scheduled independently | Both U-first and V-first transitions must reach their respective first-`Bad` monitors. Neither monitor repeats its attempt or models later events; the result is a modeled counterexample, not a claim about what happens after either violation. |
| Stage success may bind output X to A or B; A is initially staged, test A/S1, then publish X using that test | Enumerate stage-success A **and** B with their actual X bindings, including both successors. The A path can pass; the B path reaches `concrete_counterexample` on subject mismatch. A declared expected A does not exclude B. If output possibilities are unknown/unbounded, broader scope is `inconclusive`; an A-only conditional result must disclose the excluded B possibility and assumption. |
| Test fails/errors then dependent publish; success-dependency controller | Failure branch blocks that dependent publish, so this branch is safe but incomplete; the whole plan may be verified only after all other branches are checked. A scheduler permitting post-failure attempts is outside the proposed v0.1 contract: `inconclusive` unless fully specified and modeled. If such an attempt relies on the failed node's nonexistent pass it is bad; if it instead uses an independent current policy-valid prior pass, test failure alone does not make that attempt bad. |
| External attestation for A absent or unverifiable; publish requires it | `needs_evidence` if required validation can be supplied, otherwise `inconclusive`; do not invent a test pass. This differs from a modeled future test pass branch. |
| Current authenticated attestation for A reports fail/error and is designated at a ready publish | `concrete_counterexample` for false pass status, or uniform whole-plan rejection; a known failure is not missing evidence. No fabricated passing record. |
| Authorized test runner produces modeled pass for A, but hypothetical issuer is known untrusted for the scope | If the contract permits that pass branch, a ready publish using it reaches `concrete_counterexample` (or whole-plan rejection); runner authorization alone cannot establish issuer trust. Unknown issuer trust yields `needs_evidence` or `inconclusive`, not a positive result. |
| Client supplies `kind: observation` or a forged attestation | `invalid_input` if a forged envelope is presented as authenticated and validation fails; otherwise it is an untrusted claim, and `needs_evidence` if valid evidence is absent. Neither is authority. |
| Matching A attestation expired or snapshot superseded | `stale_snapshot` with refs, not a positive result. Unknown age/validity requires evidence or is inconclusive. |
| Two authenticated current records disagree on the same scoped test-run fact | `invalid_input` for contradictory premises; don't select the favorable pass. Distinct runs with a policy-defined aggregation require a reviewed rule. |
| A has a fresh authenticated external pass for S2 but policy requires S1 for publication; S2 is registered | `concrete_counterexample` if the ready publish attempts to use S2. Unknown/unregistered suite is `invalid_input`; matching digest does not make S2 sufficient. This publication rule alone does not prohibit an optional S2 test: that depends on the separate test-operation policy. |
| Authenticated P2 tries publishing to D but policy authorizes only P1 | `concrete_counterexample` for the ready attempt. If identity/authorization is unavailable, `needs_evidence`, not a guessed denial or allow. |
| Authorized P2 publishes A using P1's otherwise valid attestation | If a bound policy explicitly denies cross-principal use, `concrete_counterexample` for the attempt or uniform whole-plan rejection. If policy explicitly permits it and tenant, audience, destination and scope are compatible, this condition can hold; absent a defined sharing rule, `needs_evidence` or `inconclusive`, not implicit sharing. |
| P1 uses P1's attestation with incompatible tenant or audience | Known incompatibility makes the ready publish a `concrete_counterexample` or whole-plan rejection; principal equality alone does not override the scope checks. |
| Cyclic dependencies, missing dependency, duplicate producer, unknown operation | `invalid_input`; there is no valid graph or proof about a pruned plan. |
| Fully specified failure leaves every remaining node blocked and no transition permitted | A complete graph can yield `verified_within_model` for safety, not successful completion. An unexplained pending/no-ready stall or an unmodeled liveness request is `inconclusive`. |
| Unsupported digest algorithm, incompatible pinned version or suite definition | `invalid_input` for an unsupported encoding/version; for a recognized but policy-inapplicable version, ready use is a `concrete_counterexample` or uniform whole-plan rejection. Never coerce identities or silently reuse old evidence. |
| Horizon zero | A complete zero-attempt prefix can yield `verified_within_model` only for that vacuous prefix, explicitly saying no attempts were checked; a full-plan request with work beyond zero is `inconclusive`. |
| Publish failure with partial side effect is possible but only no-effect failure is specified | `inconclusive` for the requested coverage. An explicitly restricted no-effect-only result must disclose that exclusion and cannot be described as unqualified coverage. |
| Two independent ready actions, one can change a fact required by publish | Enumerate both orders if the change is represented: a reachable unsafe attempt is `concrete_counterexample`. If the effect, true concurrent overlap or relevant race is not represented, broader-scope result is `inconclusive`; one favorable ordering is not enough. |
| Required graph exceeds 128 states or 512 edges; or checking budget exhausted | `inconclusive` with the limit; never verify a truncated graph. At a deliberate horizon before a bad action, only a separately labeled bounded-prefix result is possible; a full-plan request remains inconclusive. |

## Open decisions and review/falsification gates

Owner review must settle digest algorithms/canonical bytes and namespaces; collector authentication, key rotation/revocation and time authority; policy language and suite applicability; cross-principal evidence sharing, tenant and audience rules; contracts and evidence for stage output identity and nondeterminism; output/attestation compatibility across snapshot revisions; scheduler failure disposition and atomicity; allowed horizon claims; authority and provenance of future runtime observations; and schema/result migration. No proposed choice above is an owner-approved policy. A future implementation must specify how policy exceptions and multiple required suites interact rather than defaulting to allow.

Falsify the design/implementation if a ready unsafe publish is omitted, a failure or schedule yields an unseen bad trace, a prediction mints evidence, an attestation crosses subject/suite/version/freshness boundaries or a principal boundary without an explicit policy-permitted sharing relation and compatible tenant, audience, destination and scope, graph caps truncate to safety, or a witness cannot replay. Require Rust-native independent raw-domain exhaustive reachability comparisons on small generated DAGs, labeled witness replay, mutation tests removing each precondition/edge/initial/bad state, permutation/reproducibility tests, stale/conflict/forgery and limit tests. These are future acceptance gates, not reported results. Independent review, CI tied to the design revision, actual tool-contract evidence and eventual real-world completeness arguments remain separate from this proposal.
