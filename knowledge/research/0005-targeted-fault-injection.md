## Interpretation and boundaries

The branch-loss experiment demonstrates that the named old integration test and the finite xtask corpus **did not detect that exact injected implementation fault**. It does not show that the unmodified verifier has this fault, that every other test missed it, or that the fault produces a false-safe public result. The direct partition fixture tests the intended all-successor semantics without depending on which partitions the coarse-start loop reaches.

Likewise, the earlier work-cutoff test derived its cutoff from a completed run and therefore did not ensure replay itself consumed budget under the injected bypass. The new tests derive the expected prefix from search and concretization separately, compare the completed run's work to that prefix plus separately replayed work, then put work/cancellation at the first replay tick. A separate nonzero witness fixture interrupts in the transition scan with an independently specified tick count, both by direct replay and via the public verifier after the fixture's first refinement. These checks explicitly couple the tests to the current `run` ordering and tick accounting; a legitimate change may require revisiting the tests, not weakening the rule that an interrupted replay is inconclusive. Neither constitutes a general proof of where every callback occurred.

Rejected alternatives: assuming a green exhaustive corpus proves all intermediate invariants, changing production semantics to fit a test, hiding surviving mutants, or introducing a permanent external mutation framework. No raw terminal output was committed or uploaded as an Actions artifact; the exact synthetic mutations, commands, failure fixtures and outcomes above are the durable reproduction record. CI for the follow-up tests must be checked separately. This is not exhaustive mutation testing or a machine-checked proof. A regression passing under its corresponding targeted mutation, or failing on restored code, would falsify the corresponding sensitivity claim.
